CMA USA Part 2

Investment Decisions

A complete CMA exam-focused theory guide covering capital budgeting, investment evaluation methods, project cash flows, risk analysis, and strategic investment decisions.

1. Introduction to Investment Decisions

Investment decisions involve selecting long-term projects that use significant company resources and are expected to generate future benefits.

The objective of investment analysis is to select projects that increase shareholder value.

Examples of Investment Decisions

  • Purchasing new equipment
  • Expanding production facilities
  • Launching new products
  • Replacing outdated technology
  • Entering new markets

2. Capital Budgeting Process

Capital budgeting is the process of evaluating and selecting long-term investments.

Main Steps

  • Identify investment opportunities
  • Estimate future cash flows
  • Evaluate project profitability
  • Select projects
  • Monitor project performance

3. Relevant Cash Flows in Investment Decisions

Investment decisions are based on incremental cash flows, which are additional cash flows caused by accepting a project.

Initial Investment

Initial investment includes the cash required to start a project.

Examples:
  • Purchase cost of equipment
  • Installation costs
  • Training costs
  • Increase in working capital

Operating Cash Flows

Operating cash flows represent additional cash generated during the project.

Formula:
Operating Cash Flow = Net Income + Depreciation

Terminal Cash Flow

Terminal cash flow occurs at the end of the project.

Includes:
  • Salvage value of assets
  • Recovery of working capital
  • Final project costs

4. Time Value of Money in Capital Budgeting

Future cash flows must be converted into present values because money received in the future is worth less than money received today.

Formula:
Present Value = Future Cash Flow ÷ (1 + r)n

5. Net Present Value (NPV)

Net Present Value measures the difference between the present value of cash inflows and the initial investment.

Formula:
NPV = Present Value of Cash Inflows − Initial Investment

Decision Rule

  • Positive NPV → Accept project
  • Negative NPV → Reject project
  • NPV of zero → Indifferent

NPV is considered one of the strongest capital budgeting methods because it directly measures value creation.

6. Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

IRR is the discount rate that makes the NPV of a project equal to zero.

Formula:
NPV = 0 at IRR

Decision Rule

  • IRR greater than required return → Accept
  • IRR lower than required return → Reject

7. Payback Period

Payback period measures the time required to recover the initial investment.

Formula:
Payback Period = Initial Investment ÷ Annual Cash Flow

Advantages

  • Simple to calculate
  • Focuses on liquidity
  • Useful for risky projects

Disadvantages

  • Ignores time value of money
  • Ignores cash flows after payback

8. Discounted Payback Period

Discounted payback adjusts the payback method by considering the time value of money.

It measures how long it takes to recover the investment using discounted cash flows.

9. Accounting Rate of Return (ARR)

ARR evaluates investment profitability using accounting income rather than cash flows.

Formula:
ARR = Average Annual Accounting Income ÷ Average Investment

ARR is simple but does not consider the time value of money.

10. Independent and Mutually Exclusive Projects

Independent Projects

Projects that do not affect each other. Multiple projects can be accepted if they meet investment requirements.

Mutually Exclusive Projects

Projects where choosing one prevents choosing another.

Example:

Selecting one of two different production machines.

11. Working Capital Considerations

Investment projects may require additional working capital during operations.

Important rule:
Increase in Working Capital = Cash Outflow
Recovery of Working Capital = Cash Inflow

12. Risk Analysis in Investment Decisions

Investment decisions involve uncertainty because future cash flows are estimates.

Risk analysis methods:
  • Sensitivity analysis
  • Scenario analysis
  • Simulation analysis
  • Risk-adjusted discount rates

13. Sensitivity Analysis

Sensitivity analysis examines how changes in one variable affect project results.

Examples:
  • Sales volume changes
  • Cost increases
  • Interest rate changes

14. Scenario Analysis

Scenario analysis evaluates multiple possible outcomes.

Examples:
  • Best case scenario
  • Expected scenario
  • Worst case scenario

15. Risk-Adjusted Investment Decisions

Projects with higher risk may require a higher expected return.

Methods:
  • Higher discount rate
  • Probability analysis
  • Expected value approach

17. CMA Essay Answer Framework

For investment decision essays:

1. Identify the investment opportunity.
2. Calculate relevant cash flows.
3. Apply evaluation method (NPV/IRR).
4. Consider risk factors.
5. Recommend accept or reject decision.
