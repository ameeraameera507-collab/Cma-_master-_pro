CMA USA Part 2

Risk Management

A complete CMA exam-focused guide covering risk identification, measurement, mitigation strategies, enterprise risk management, and financial risk concepts.

1. Introduction to Risk Management

Risk management is the process of identifying, analyzing, responding to, and monitoring risks that may affect an organization's objectives.

The goal of risk management is not to eliminate all risks, but to manage risks within acceptable limits while creating value.

2. Types of Risk

Business Risk

Business risk arises from uncertainty in normal operations and the ability of a company to generate profits.

Examples:
  • Changes in customer demand
  • Competition
  • Operational problems
  • Economic conditions

Financial Risk

Financial risk results from the use of debt financing and obligations to creditors.

Examples:
  • Interest rate changes
  • High debt levels
  • Currency fluctuations

Operational Risk

Operational risk comes from failures in internal processes, people, systems, or external events.

Examples:
  • System failures
  • Employee errors
  • Fraud
  • Supply chain disruptions

3. Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)

Enterprise Risk Management is a company-wide approach to identifying and managing risks across all departments.

Objectives of ERM

  • Protect company assets
  • Improve decision-making
  • Reduce unexpected losses
  • Support strategic objectives
  • Improve risk awareness

4. COSO Enterprise Risk Management Framework

The COSO ERM framework provides guidance for organizations to integrate risk management with strategy and performance.

Major components:
  • Governance and Culture
  • Strategy and Objective Setting
  • Performance
  • Review and Revision
  • Information, Communication and Reporting

5. Risk Identification

Risk identification determines possible events that may prevent an organization from achieving its objectives.

Methods:
  • Internal audits
  • Management reviews
  • Scenario analysis
  • Historical data analysis
  • Employee feedback

6. Risk Assessment

After identifying risks, organizations evaluate their likelihood and potential impact.

Risk assessment considers:
  • Probability of occurrence
  • Financial impact
  • Operational effect
  • Strategic consequences

7. Risk Response Strategies

Risk Avoidance

Removing activities that create unacceptable risk.

Example: Stopping production of a dangerous product.

Risk Reduction

Taking actions to reduce the probability or impact of risk.

Examples:
  • Internal controls
  • Employee training
  • Quality procedures

Risk Sharing

Transferring part of the risk to another party.

Examples:
  • Insurance
  • Outsourcing
  • Hedging

Risk Acceptance

Accepting risk when the cost of controlling it exceeds the expected benefit.

8. Insurance as Risk Management

Insurance transfers certain risks from a company to an insurance provider.

Common insurance types:
  • Property insurance
  • Liability insurance
  • Business interruption insurance
  • Employee-related insurance

9. Hedging Financial Risk

Hedging reduces exposure to unfavorable changes in financial variables.

Common risks:
  • Foreign currency risk
  • Interest rate risk
  • Commodity price risk

10. Foreign Exchange Risk

Companies operating internationally face currency exchange uncertainty.

Types:
  • Transaction exposure
  • Translation exposure
  • Economic exposure

11. Interest Rate Risk

Interest rate risk occurs when changes in market interest rates affect borrowing costs or investment returns.

Management methods:
  • Fixed-rate financing
  • Interest rate swaps
  • Diversification

12. Fraud Risk Management

Organizations must establish controls to prevent and detect fraud.

Fraud prevention methods:
  • Segregation of duties
  • Authorization controls
  • Internal audits
  • Ethical culture
  • Whistleblower systems

13. Internal Controls and Risk

Strong internal controls reduce operational and financial risks.

Examples:
  • Access restrictions
  • Reconciliations
  • Approval procedures
  • Monitoring activities

14. Risk Monitoring

Risk management is a continuous process requiring regular review.

Monitoring includes:
  • Performance measurement
  • Risk reports
  • Control testing
  • Management review

15. CMA Exam Focus Areas

  • ERM concepts
  • COSO framework
  • Risk response methods
  • Financial risk management
  • Hedging concepts
  • Internal controls
  • Fraud prevention

16. CMA Essay Answer Framework

For risk management essays:

1. Identify the risk.
2. Explain possible impact.
3. Recommend risk response.
4. Discuss controls or monitoring.
5. Explain business benefits.

17. Quick Revision Notes

ERM = Company-wide risk management

Avoid = Remove risk

Reduce = Lower probability or impact

Share = Transfer risk

Accept = Keep risk when reasonable
