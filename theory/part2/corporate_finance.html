CMA USA Part 2

Corporate Finance

A complete CMA exam-focused theory guide covering financing decisions, capital structure, cost of capital, working capital management, and financial strategy.

1. Introduction to Corporate Finance

Corporate finance focuses on how organizations raise funds, invest resources, manage financial risks, and make decisions that increase shareholder value.

The primary goal of financial management is maximizing the long-term value of the organization.

Main Responsibilities of Finance Managers

  • Investment decisions
  • Financing decisions
  • Working capital management
  • Risk management
  • Financial planning

2. Financial Management Objectives

Companies must balance profitability, risk, and liquidity while making financial decisions.

Major Objectives

  • Increase shareholder wealth
  • Maintain adequate cash flow
  • Control financial risk
  • Use resources efficiently
  • Support business growth

3. Time Value of Money (TVM)

The time value of money means that money available today is worth more than the same amount received in the future because current funds can earn returns.

Future Value

FV = PV × (1 + r)n
Where:
  • FV = Future Value
  • PV = Present Value
  • r = Interest rate
  • n = Number of periods

Present Value

PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)n

4. Risk and Return Relationship

Financial decisions involve a relationship between expected return and risk.

Generally, higher expected returns require accepting higher levels of risk.

Types of Risk

  • Business Risk: Risk from company operations.
  • Financial Risk: Risk created by debt financing.
  • Market Risk: Risk from overall market changes.

5. Cost of Capital

Cost of capital represents the required return expected by investors who provide funding to the company.

Companies use cost of capital when evaluating investments and financing decisions.

Cost of Debt

After-tax Cost of Debt = Interest Rate × (1 − Tax Rate)

Debt is adjusted for taxes because interest expense usually provides a tax benefit.

Cost of Equity

Cost of equity is the return required by shareholders for investing in the company.

6. Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)

WACC represents the average cost of all sources of financing.

WACC = (Debt Weight × Cost of Debt) + (Equity Weight × Cost of Equity)

WACC is commonly used as a discount rate in investment decisions.

7. Capital Structure Decisions

Capital structure refers to the combination of debt and equity used to finance company operations.

Debt Financing Advantages

  • Interest tax deduction
  • Lower financing cost
  • No ownership dilution

Debt Financing Disadvantages

  • Required interest payments
  • Higher financial risk
  • Possible financial distress

Equity Financing Advantages

  • No mandatory repayment
  • Lower bankruptcy risk
  • Improves financial flexibility

Equity Financing Disadvantages

  • Ownership dilution
  • Higher cost compared with debt

8. Dividend Policy

Dividend policy determines how much profit is distributed to shareholders and how much is retained for future growth.

Factors affecting dividend decisions:
  • Cash availability
  • Growth opportunities
  • Investor expectations
  • Debt agreements

9. Working Capital Management

Working capital management focuses on managing short-term assets and liabilities to maintain liquidity and operational efficiency.

Formula:
Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities

Components of Working Capital

  • Cash
  • Accounts receivable
  • Inventory
  • Accounts payable

10. Cash Management

Cash management ensures that a company has enough cash to operate while avoiding excess unused funds.

Objectives:
  • Maintain liquidity
  • Reduce unnecessary cash balances
  • Improve investment returns

11. Receivables Management

Receivables management controls credit sales and collection of customer payments.

Important considerations:
  • Credit standards
  • Credit terms
  • Collection policies
  • Bad debt risk

12. Inventory Management

Inventory management balances product availability with the cost of holding inventory.

Costs of inventory:
  • Storage costs
  • Insurance costs
  • Obsolescence risk
  • Opportunity cost

13. Short-Term Financing

Short-term financing provides funds needed for daily operations.

Examples:
  • Trade credit
  • Bank loans
  • Commercial paper
  • Lines of credit

14. Long-Term Financing

Long-term financing supports major investments and expansion.

Examples:
  • Long-term debt
  • Bonds
  • Common stock
  • Preferred stock

15. CMA Exam Focus Areas

16. CMA Essay Answer Framework

For corporate finance essay questions:

1. Identify the financial decision.
2. Explain the objective.
3. Apply formulas when required.
4. Discuss risk and business impact.
5. Provide management recommendation.
