CMA USA Part 2

Decision Analysis

A complete CMA exam-focused theory guide covering relevant costs, short-term decisions, pricing, product decisions, constraints, and strategic analysis.

1. Introduction to Decision Analysis

Decision analysis helps managers choose the best alternative by comparing financial and non-financial consequences of different options.

Management accountants provide information that helps decision-makers evaluate costs, benefits, risks, and opportunities.

Decision Analysis Process

  • Identify the problem
  • Determine possible alternatives
  • Collect relevant information
  • Analyze costs and benefits
  • Select the best alternative
  • Review the results

2. Relevant Costs and Revenues

Relevant costs are future costs that differ between alternatives and affect a decision.

A cost is relevant when:
  • It occurs in the future
  • It differs between choices
Examples:
  • Additional labor cost
  • Additional material cost
  • Avoidable fixed costs

3. Sunk Costs

Sunk costs are costs that have already occurred and cannot be changed by any future decision.

Sunk costs should be ignored when making decisions.

Example:
  • Original purchase cost of old equipment
  • Previous research expenses

4. Opportunity Costs

Opportunity cost represents the benefit lost when choosing one alternative over another.

Example:

If a company uses a building for production instead of renting it out, the lost rental income is an opportunity cost.

5. Make-or-Buy Decisions

Companies often decide whether to produce a product internally or purchase it from an outside supplier.

Relevant Factors

  • Purchase price
  • Avoidable production costs
  • Available production capacity
  • Quality considerations
  • Supplier reliability
Decision rule:
Choose the option with the lowest relevant cost while maintaining quality.

6. Special Order Decisions

A special order is an additional order usually offered at a reduced price.

A company should accept when:
  • There is unused capacity
  • Additional revenue exceeds additional costs
  • Normal sales are not negatively affected
Formula:
Incremental Profit = Additional Revenue − Additional Costs

7. Product Mix Decisions

Product mix decisions determine which products should receive limited resources.

When resources are limited, managers focus on:
  • Contribution margin per unit
  • Contribution margin per limiting factor
Formula:
Contribution Margin per Constraint = Contribution Margin ÷ Units of Limited Resource

8. Constraint Analysis

A constraint is any limitation that prevents an organization from achieving its maximum output.

Examples:
  • Limited machine hours
  • Limited labor hours
  • Limited materials
  • Production capacity

9. Contribution Margin Approach

Contribution margin shows how much revenue remains after variable costs to cover fixed costs and generate profit.

Formula:
Contribution Margin = Sales − Variable Costs
Contribution Margin Ratio:
CM Ratio = Contribution Margin ÷ Sales

10. Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) Analysis

CVP analysis examines the relationship between costs, sales volume, and profit.

Break-Even Point in Units

Break-even Units = Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution Margin per Unit

Break-Even Sales Dollars

Break-even Sales = Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution Margin Ratio

11. Pricing Decisions

Pricing decisions consider costs, customer demand, competition, and strategic objectives.

Cost-Based Pricing

Price is determined by adding a markup to cost.

Formula:
Selling Price = Cost + Markup

Market-Based Pricing

Price is determined by customer demand and competitive conditions.

12. Target Costing

Target costing begins with the market price and determines the maximum allowable cost needed to achieve desired profit.

Formula:
Target Cost = Market Price − Desired Profit

13. Life-Cycle Costing

Life-cycle costing considers all costs associated with a product from design through disposal.

Stages include:
  • Research and development
  • Design
  • Production
  • Marketing
  • Distribution
  • Customer service

14. Capital Investment Decisions

Capital investment decisions evaluate long-term projects requiring significant resources.

Common methods:
  • Net Present Value (NPV)
  • Internal Rate of Return (IRR)
  • Payback Period
  • Accounting Rate of Return

15. CMA Exam Focus Areas

  • Relevant cost identification
  • Sunk cost treatment
  • Opportunity cost analysis
  • Make-or-buy decisions
  • Special orders
  • Contribution margin analysis
  • Constraint decisions
  • Target costing

16. CMA Essay Answer Framework

For decision analysis essays:

1. Identify the alternatives.
2. Separate relevant and irrelevant costs.
3. Calculate financial impact.
4. Consider qualitative factors.
5. Recommend the best decision.

17. Quick Revision Notes

Relevant Cost = Future + Different between alternatives

Sunk Cost = Ignore

Opportunity Cost = Benefit sacrificed

CM = Sales − Variable Cost
