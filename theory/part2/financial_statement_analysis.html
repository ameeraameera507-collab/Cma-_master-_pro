CMA USA Part 2

Financial Statement Analysis

A complete CMA exam-focused theory guide covering financial statements, financial ratios, performance evaluation, interpretation of financial data, and strategic decision support.

1. Introduction to Financial Statement Analysis

Financial statement analysis is the process of examining financial reports to evaluate a company's financial health, profitability, efficiency, liquidity, and future performance.

Management accountants use financial analysis to support planning, control, and strategic decision-making.

Main Objectives of Analysis

  • Evaluate profitability
  • Measure liquidity and solvency
  • Identify strengths and weaknesses
  • Compare current performance with past periods
  • Support business decisions

2. Major Financial Statements

Income Statement

The income statement reports revenues, expenses, and profit or loss over a specific period.

Formula:
Revenue − Expenses = Net Income

Balance Sheet

The balance sheet shows the financial position of a company at a specific date.

Formula:
Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Statement of Cash Flows

Shows cash inflows and outflows from operating, investing, and financing activities.

3. Horizontal Analysis

Horizontal analysis compares financial statement amounts across different periods to identify changes and trends.

Formula:
Percentage Change = (Current Year Amount − Previous Year Amount) ÷ Previous Year Amount × 100
Used for:
  • Revenue growth analysis
  • Expense trend analysis
  • Profitability changes

4. Vertical Analysis

Vertical analysis expresses financial statement items as percentages of a base amount.

Examples:
  • Income statement items are usually compared to sales.
  • Balance sheet items are usually compared to total assets.

5. Ratio Analysis

Financial ratios convert accounting information into useful measures for comparison and decision-making.

The major categories are:
  • Liquidity ratios
  • Solvency ratios
  • Profitability ratios
  • Activity ratios
  • Market ratios

6. Liquidity Ratios

Liquidity ratios measure the company's ability to meet short-term obligations.

Current Ratio

Formula:
Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

A higher current ratio generally indicates stronger short-term financial capacity.

Quick Ratio (Acid-Test Ratio)

Formula:
Quick Ratio = (Current Assets − Inventory) ÷ Current Liabilities

Inventory is removed because it may take longer to convert into cash.

7. Solvency Ratios

Solvency ratios measure the company's ability to meet long-term obligations.

Debt-to-Equity Ratio

Formula:
Debt-to-Equity = Total Debt ÷ Total Equity

A higher ratio indicates greater reliance on borrowed funds.

Times Interest Earned

Formula:
Times Interest Earned = EBIT ÷ Interest Expense

Measures the company's ability to pay interest obligations.

8. Profitability Ratios

Profitability ratios measure the company's ability to generate earnings.

Gross Profit Margin

Formula:
Gross Profit Margin = Gross Profit ÷ Sales × 100

Net Profit Margin

Formula:
Net Profit Margin = Net Income ÷ Sales × 100

Return on Assets (ROA)

Formula:
ROA = Net Income ÷ Average Total Assets

Return on Equity (ROE)

Formula:
ROE = Net Income ÷ Average Equity

9. Activity Ratios

Activity ratios evaluate how efficiently a company uses its assets.

Inventory Turnover

Formula:
Inventory Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold ÷ Average Inventory

Accounts Receivable Turnover

Formula:
Receivable Turnover = Sales ÷ Average Accounts Receivable

10. DuPont Analysis

DuPont analysis breaks return on equity into key components to understand what drives profitability.

Formula:
ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Financial Leverage
The three drivers are:
  • Profitability
  • Efficiency
  • Use of debt financing

11. Common Size Financial Statements

Common size statements allow comparison between companies of different sizes.

Examples:
  • Sales as 100% of revenue analysis
  • Assets as percentage of total assets

12. Limitations of Financial Statement Analysis

  • Historical information may not predict future results
  • Accounting methods may differ between companies
  • Inflation can distort comparisons
  • Ratios require industry comparison
  • Management estimates affect results

13. CMA Exam Focus Areas

  • Liquidity ratios
  • Profitability ratios
  • Solvency analysis
  • DuPont analysis
  • Horizontal and vertical analysis
  • Financial statement interpretation
  • Effects of accounting choices

14. CMA Essay Answer Framework

When explaining financial analysis in the CMA exam:

1. Identify the ratio or analysis method.
2. Provide the formula.
3. Explain what the result means.
4. Explain how management uses the information.

15. Quick Revision Notes

Liquidity = Ability to pay short-term debts

Solvency = Ability to survive long-term

Profitability = Ability to generate income

Efficiency = How well assets are used

ROE = Profitability × Efficiency × Leverage
