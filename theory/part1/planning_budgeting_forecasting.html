CMA Part 1

Planning, Budgeting & Forecasting

A complete CMA USA preparation chapter covering strategic planning, budget development, forecasting techniques, and management decision-making.

1. Chapter Overview

Planning, budgeting, and forecasting are essential management accounting activities used by organizations to convert strategic goals into measurable financial and operational plans.

A CMA professional must understand how managers create budgets, evaluate performance, allocate resources, and adjust plans when business conditions change.

2. Strategic Planning

Strategic planning is the process of defining an organization's long-term direction and deciding how resources will be used to achieve competitive advantages.

Key Elements of Strategic Planning

  • Mission Statement - explains why the organization exists.
  • Vision Statement - describes the future direction of the organization.
  • Objectives - measurable goals used to evaluate success.
  • Strategies - actions selected to achieve objectives.

3. Relationship Between Strategy and Budgeting

The budget translates strategic plans into specific financial targets. A company cannot effectively execute strategy without allocating resources through budgeting.

Strategy → Budget → Performance Measurement

Strategic goals create operating plans. Operating plans become budgets. Actual results are compared against budgets to evaluate performance.

4. Budget Concepts

A budget is a formal quantitative plan showing expected revenues, expenses, resources, and financial results for a future period.

Purposes of Budgeting

  • Planning future activities
  • Coordinating different departments
  • Communicating organizational goals
  • Controlling costs
  • Evaluating employee and manager performance

5. Master Budget

The master budget is a complete collection of operating and financial budgets prepared for a specific period.

Master Budget Flow

Sales Budget
↓
Production Budget
↓
Direct Material Budget
↓
Direct Labor Budget
↓
Manufacturing Overhead Budget
↓
Cash Budget
↓
Budgeted Financial Statements

6. Sales Budget

The sales budget estimates expected sales revenue and sales volume. It is normally the starting point of the master budget because production and resource requirements depend on expected sales.

Formula:

Expected Sales Revenue = Expected Units Sold × Selling Price per Unit

7. Production Budget

The production budget determines how many units must be manufactured to meet sales requirements while maintaining desired inventory levels.

Units to Produce = Budgeted Sales Units + Desired Ending Inventory − Beginning Inventory

8. Flexible Budget

A flexible budget adjusts budgeted costs according to actual activity levels. It provides a better performance comparison because it separates the impact of volume changes from management performance.

Flexible Budget Advantages

  • More accurate performance evaluation
  • Useful for cost control
  • Highlights efficiency problems

9. Static Budget

A static budget is prepared for one expected activity level and does not change when actual activity changes.

Static budgets are useful for planning but less effective for performance evaluation when actual production differs from expectations.

10. Rolling Forecasts

A rolling forecast continuously updates future expectations by adding a new period when the current period ends.

Example: A 12-month forecast always maintains a future 12-month outlook.

11. Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB)

Zero-based budgeting requires managers to justify all expenses from the beginning rather than automatically using previous year spending.

Advantages

  • Reduces unnecessary spending
  • Encourages critical evaluation
  • Improves resource allocation

12. Activity-Based Budgeting

Activity-based budgeting uses expected activities and cost drivers to develop budgets.

It focuses on understanding why costs occur instead of simply increasing previous budgets.

13. Forecasting Techniques

Time Series Analysis

Uses historical data patterns to predict future results.

Moving Average

Uses average results from previous periods to smooth fluctuations.

Regression Analysis

Analyzes the relationship between dependent and independent variables to predict future outcomes.

14. Budget Behavioral Issues

Budgets influence employee behavior. Poorly designed budgets can create negative behavior such as budget manipulation or unrealistic targets.

  • Budgetary slack
  • Unrealistic expectations
  • Short-term decision focus
