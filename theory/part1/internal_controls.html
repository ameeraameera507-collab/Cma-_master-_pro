CMA USA Part 1

Internal Controls

A complete CMA exam-focused theory guide covering internal control systems, risk management, fraud prevention, governance, and control activities.

1. Purpose of Internal Controls

Internal controls are policies, procedures, and processes designed by an organization to provide reasonable assurance that objectives are achieved.

A strong internal control system helps organizations protect assets, produce reliable information, improve operations, and comply with laws and regulations.

Main Objectives of Internal Controls

  • Protect company assets
  • Ensure accuracy and reliability of financial information
  • Improve operational efficiency
  • Ensure compliance with laws and regulations
  • Reduce fraud and errors

2. COSO Internal Control Framework

The COSO framework is one of the most widely used frameworks for designing and evaluating internal control systems.

COSO consists of five integrated components.

Five Components of COSO

1. Control Environment

The control environment represents the overall attitude, awareness, and actions of management regarding internal controls.

Important factors:
  • Management integrity and ethical values
  • Board oversight
  • Organizational structure
  • Assignment of authority and responsibility
  • Employee competence

2. Risk Assessment

Risk assessment identifies and analyzes risks that may prevent an organization from achieving its objectives.

Organizations evaluate both internal and external risks.

Examples:
  • Fraud risk
  • Technology failures
  • Market changes
  • Regulatory changes

3. Control Activities

Control activities are specific actions designed to reduce risks and ensure objectives are achieved.

Examples:
  • Authorization procedures
  • Approvals
  • Reconciliations
  • Physical controls
  • Segregation of duties

4. Information and Communication

Organizations must collect and communicate relevant information to employees who need it to perform their responsibilities.

Effective communication ensures:
  • Employees understand responsibilities
  • Problems are reported quickly
  • Management receives accurate information

5. Monitoring Activities

Monitoring evaluates whether internal controls continue to operate effectively.

Methods include:
  • Internal audits
  • Management reviews
  • Continuous monitoring systems

3. Preventive, Detective, and Corrective Controls

Preventive Controls

Preventive controls attempt to stop errors or fraud before they occur.

Examples:
  • Password protection
  • Authorization requirements
  • Employee background checks

Detective Controls

Detective controls identify problems after they occur.

Examples:
  • Bank reconciliations
  • Internal audits
  • Inventory counts

Corrective Controls

Corrective controls fix problems and prevent future recurrence.

Examples:
  • System repairs
  • Policy changes
  • Employee training

4. Segregation of Duties

Segregation of duties separates responsibilities among different employees to reduce the possibility of fraud and errors.

The three major responsibilities should be separated:
  • Authorization of transactions
  • Recording transactions
  • Custody of assets

One person should not control all three activities.

5. Authorization Controls

Authorization controls ensure transactions are approved by appropriate personnel.

Examples:
  • Purchase approvals
  • Payment authorization
  • Management approval limits

6. Physical Controls

Physical controls protect company assets from theft, damage, or unauthorized use.

Examples:
  • Security systems
  • Restricted access areas
  • Inventory protection
  • Asset identification tags

7. Information Technology Controls

IT controls protect information systems and ensure reliable processing of data.

General IT Controls

  • User access controls
  • Password management
  • System development controls
  • Backup and recovery procedures

Application Controls

Application controls ensure individual transactions are processed accurately.

Examples:
  • Input validation
  • Processing checks
  • Output verification

8. Fraud Prevention and Detection

Fraud is an intentional act designed to obtain an unfair or illegal benefit.

The fraud triangle includes:
  • Pressure: Financial or personal motivation
  • Opportunity: Weak controls allowing fraud
  • Rationalization: Justification of dishonest behavior

9. Internal Audit

Internal auditing is an independent evaluation activity that helps organizations improve operations and control systems.

Internal auditors:
  • Evaluate controls
  • Identify risks
  • Recommend improvements
  • Support governance

10. Corporate Governance

Corporate governance refers to the system by which organizations are directed and controlled.

Good governance requires:
  • Ethical leadership
  • Board oversight
  • Accountability
  • Transparency
