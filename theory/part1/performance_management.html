CMA Part 1

Performance Management

A detailed CMA USA preparation chapter covering responsibility accounting, performance measurement, ROI, residual income, balanced scorecards, transfer pricing, and managerial decision evaluation.

1. Chapter Overview

Performance management is the process of measuring, analyzing, and improving an organization's results. CMA professionals use performance management systems to ensure that business activities support strategic objectives.

The purpose is not only measuring financial results but also understanding the reasons behind performance and identifying improvement opportunities.

2. Responsibility Accounting

Responsibility accounting assigns specific areas of accountability to managers. Each manager is evaluated based on the factors they can control.

Types of Responsibility Centers

Cost Center

A cost center manager controls costs but does not directly control revenue or investment decisions.

Example: Production department or maintenance department.

Revenue Center

A revenue center manager is responsible mainly for generating sales revenue but usually does not control production costs.

Profit Center

A profit center manager controls both revenues and costs and is evaluated based on profitability.

Investment Center

An investment center manager controls revenues, costs, and assets invested in the business unit.

3. Performance Measurement Systems

A performance measurement system collects and evaluates information to determine whether organizational objectives are being achieved.

Effective Measurement Systems Should:

  • Support company strategy
  • Provide useful information
  • Encourage correct employee behavior
  • Balance short-term and long-term goals

4. Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

KPIs are measurable indicators used to evaluate success in important areas of business performance.

Examples of KPIs

  • Sales growth percentage
  • Customer satisfaction rate
  • Production efficiency
  • Employee retention
  • Return on investment

5. Financial Performance Measures

Return on Investment (ROI)

ROI measures how efficiently an organization uses invested assets to generate operating income.

ROI = Operating Income ÷ Average Operating Assets

ROI Components

ROI can also be analyzed using the DuPont approach.

ROI = Profit Margin × Asset Turnover

Profit margin measures profitability from sales. Asset turnover measures how efficiently assets generate sales.

6. Advantages and Limitations of ROI

Advantages

  • Easy comparison between business units
  • Encourages efficient asset usage
  • Measures overall profitability

Limitations

  • Managers may reject profitable projects if ROI decreases
  • May encourage short-term decisions
  • Different accounting methods may affect comparison

7. Residual Income

Residual income measures profit remaining after deducting the required return on invested assets.

Residual Income = Operating Income − (Required Rate of Return × Operating Assets)

Residual income encourages managers to accept projects that increase total company value even if ROI decreases.

8. ROI vs Residual Income

ROI:
Measures percentage return.

Residual Income:
Measures additional profit after capital charge.

9. Balanced Scorecard

The balanced scorecard evaluates performance from multiple perspectives rather than relying only on financial measures.

Four Perspectives

  • Financial Perspective: Profitability, revenue growth, cost control.
  • Customer Perspective: Customer satisfaction, loyalty, market share.
  • Internal Process Perspective: Quality, efficiency, production improvement.
  • Learning and Growth Perspective: Employee skills, innovation, technology improvement.

10. Transfer Pricing

Transfer pricing determines the price charged when one division transfers products or services to another division within the same company.

Objectives of Transfer Pricing

  • Evaluate divisional performance
  • Encourage goal congruence
  • Support decision making

Transfer Pricing Methods

Market-Based Transfer Price

Uses the external market price as the transfer price when a competitive market exists.

Cost-Based Transfer Price

Uses manufacturing cost as the basis for internal transfers.

Negotiated Transfer Price

Divisions negotiate a mutually acceptable transfer price.

11. Benchmarking

Benchmarking compares organizational performance with industry leaders or best practices to identify improvement opportunities.

12. Continuous Improvement

Continuous improvement focuses on constantly improving processes, reducing waste, increasing quality, and creating customer value.

  • Kaizen philosophy
  • Process improvement
  • Waste reduction
  • Quality improvement

14. Common CMA Exam Traps

  • ROI is a percentage measure; residual income is a dollar amount.
  • Investment center managers control assets.
  • Balanced scorecard includes both financial and non-financial measures.
  • Transfer pricing affects divisional performance evaluation.
