CMA USA Part 1

External Financial Reporting Decisions

Complete CMA exam-focused theory covering financial reporting concepts, financial statements, recognition, measurement, and reporting decisions.

1. Purpose of External Financial Reporting

External financial reporting provides financial information to users outside the organization. These users include investors, creditors, regulators, and other stakeholders who use financial information to make economic decisions.

Management accountants must understand external reporting because internal decisions often depend on accurate financial information.

2. Objectives of Financial Reporting

The main objective of financial reporting is to provide useful information about an entity's financial position, financial performance, and cash flows.

  • Help investors evaluate performance
  • Help creditors assess repayment ability
  • Support management decision-making
  • Improve transparency and accountability

3. The Four Main Financial Statements

Income Statement

The income statement reports revenues, expenses, gains, losses, and net income for a specific period.

Formula:

Revenue - Expenses = Net Income

Balance Sheet

The balance sheet shows the financial position of an organization at a specific point in time.

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Statement of Cash Flows

The statement of cash flows explains changes in cash during a period.

Three activities:

  • Operating activities
  • Investing activities
  • Financing activities

Statement of Changes in Equity

Shows changes in owner's equity caused by profits, losses, dividends, and additional investments.

4. Accounting Equation

The accounting equation is the foundation of accounting. Every transaction must maintain equality between resources and claims against those resources.

Assets = Liabilities + Owners' Equity

5. Assets Recognition and Measurement

An asset is a resource controlled by an organization that is expected to provide future economic benefits.

Examples of Assets

  • Cash
  • Accounts receivable
  • Inventory
  • Property, plant and equipment
  • Intangible assets

Asset Measurement Methods

  • Historical Cost: Original purchase price used for recording.
  • Fair Value: Current market-based measurement.
  • Net Realizable Value: Expected selling price less completion and selling costs.

6. Liabilities Recognition

A liability is a present obligation resulting from past events that will require future economic sacrifice.

Examples:
  • Accounts payable
  • Loans payable
  • Bonds payable
  • Accrued expenses

7. Revenue Recognition

Revenue recognition determines when revenue should be recorded in financial statements.

Revenue is generally recognized when the company satisfies its obligation to provide goods or services.

Five-Step Revenue Recognition Model

  1. Identify the contract with the customer
  2. Identify performance obligations
  3. Determine transaction price
  4. Allocate price to performance obligations
  5. Recognize revenue when obligations are satisfied

8. Expense Recognition and Matching Principle

Expenses should be recognized in the same period as the revenues they help generate.

This principle ensures accurate measurement of profit.

9. Inventory Accounting

Inventory represents goods held for sale or materials used in production.

Inventory Cost Methods

  • FIFO: First-in, first-out assumes oldest inventory is sold first.
  • LIFO: Last-in, first-out assumes newest inventory is sold first.
  • Weighted Average: Uses average inventory cost.

Inventory decisions affect:

  • Cost of goods sold
  • Gross profit
  • Net income
  • Total assets

10. Depreciation

Depreciation allocates the cost of long-term assets over their useful life.

Common Methods

  • Straight-line depreciation
  • Units-of-production method
  • Declining balance method

Straight-line Depreciation = (Cost - Residual Value) ÷ Useful Life

11. Intangible Assets

Intangible assets are non-physical assets that provide future economic benefits.

Examples:
  • Patents
  • Copyrights
  • Trademarks
  • Goodwill

12. GAAP and IFRS

GAAP and IFRS are accounting frameworks used to prepare financial statements.

  • US GAAP: Accounting principles primarily used in the United States.
  • IFRS: International accounting standards used globally.
